Fake banking platforms impersonate legitimate financial institutions and offer online banking services, payment facilities, account management, loans, transfers, or other financial services in order to defraud consumers. The promotion of financial services is subject to regulation in many countries. Financial institutions and online banking services may be required to comply with applicable laws and regulations governing financial services, consumer protection, data protection, and electronic transactions.

Fraudulent banking websites may exhibit characteristics that can help confirm their fraudulent nature, including but not limited to the following:

* Claims of being an established financial institution without verifiable authorization or registration.
* Requests for sensitive banking credentials, payment information, or personal identification without adequate security or legitimate business justification.
* Misleading claims regarding account balances, deposits, transfers, loans, or other financial services.
* Use of banking names, logos, certificates, or regulatory information belonging to legitimate organizations without authorization.
* Claims that the company is authorised by one or more regulatory agencies when no such authorisation exists.
* No verifiable physical address, telephone number, or legitimate customer-support information.
* Website claims that the company has many years of financial or banking experience despite evidence suggesting that the website or domain is recently established.
* Use of misleading testimonials or claims intended to create a false impression of legitimacy.
